# 06-1 — Management of information assets

**Category:** 06
**Source version:** 2026-01-01
**Current maturity:** not_regularly_reviewed
**Target maturity:** Regularly Reviewed
**Last reviewed:** never
**Next review due:** not scheduled

## MCNA Position

**Regularly Reviewed:** Information asset management policies and proper asset storage (including retention and archival) and are regularly reviewed

## Control Statement

Information assets are managed in accordance with their importance classification, and are maintained and disposed of in adherence to defined and expected procedures

## Recommended Actions

| ID | Action | Status | Owner |
|----|--------|--------|-------|
| 06-1-a | Define information asset management policies based on their lifecycle (acquisition, use, storage, disposal) | not_started | — |
| 06-1-b | Identify the information assets to be protected and set the level of importance in terms of the degree of impact on internal and external parties in the event of an incident (e.g., data leaks or breaches, falsified data or company branding used wrongfully, etc.) | not_started | — |
| 06-1-c | Define permitted scope of use, retention periods, persons responsible or ownership, and handling methods (storage, disposal, transfer) appropriate to the importance level or sensitivity classification of information assets | not_started | — |
| 06-1-d | Physical information media such as paper and other media, as well as electronic data such as files, should be labeled according to their classification | not_started | — |
| 06-1-e | Permit the use of designated external media only when necessary, and restrict export-capable endpoints | not_started | — |
| 06-1-f | When making information publicly available, such as publishing on the organization's website, etc., establish rules to ensure that the disclosed information does not contain confidential information (e.g., review and approval by multiple people) | not_started | — |
| 06-1-g | Manage information assets following established information asset management policies; maintain a current inventory of assets (e.g., in the form of a list, ledger, database etc.), and include information on the asset's importance level; record activities or interactions with the asset (e.g. when it is viewed, altered, and deleted or disposed) | not_started | — |
| 06-1-h | Store information assets in appropriate locations depending on the type of data (e.g., personally identifiable information, protected health information, financial account numbers, etc.) and include metadata such as storage location and ownership | not_started | — |
| 06-1-i | Restrict access to sensitive data and other important data so that only designated users and systems can access them, and obtain access records | not_started | — |
| 06-1-j | Important external storage media and disks are managed under lock and key, and/or managed under a check-out/check-in system so that they are tracked when they are brought out/returned | not_started | — |
| 06-1-k | Acquire a log related to the use of the external storage device and periodically review it | not_started | — |
| 06-1-l | Periodically delete unnecessary data and data that has passed the protection period stipulated by law, and update the ledger/list in which that data is tracked | not_started | — |
| 06-1-m | For critical information, visualize data flow including contractors | not_started | — |
| 06-1-n | When storing important information (such as personal information), mask the data as necessary | not_started | — |
| 06-1-o | Periodically review the asset management policies; review policy effectiveness in terms of actual compliance | not_started | — |
| 06-1-p | Regularly check for unmanaged personal / confidential information, such as by using a tool | not_started | — |

## Insufficient Measures Risks

Damage from adverse events such as internal fraud, misconduct, or other wrongdoing, or from cyber-attacks can be magnified because important information can be easily removed or tampered with. Inability to meet the requirements (such as those surrounding confidentiality) for "trade secrets" under the Unfair Competition Prevention Law (Japan, or similar requirements elsewhere) and prevent the legal protection of proprietary technologies, knowhow, and other information.

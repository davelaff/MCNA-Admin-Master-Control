# 19-4 — Formation of incident response teams

**Source version:** 2026-01-01

## MCNA Position

**Regularly Reviewed:** Organization members, and their roles and responsibilities are periodically reviewed

## Control Statement

Composition of a specialized team for handling incidents, defining roles in emergencies and normal times

## Recommended Actions

| ID | Action | Status | Owner |
|----|--------|--------|-------|
| 19-4-a | Understand and formulate skills necessary for conducting root cause analysis for incidents, responding to them, and for studying measures to prevent a recurrence and select corresponding personnel and form a specialized team | not_started | — |
| 19-4-b | Create an incident response flow diagram or chart showing not only the person in charge of technical response but also all relevant teams and departments (Relevant departments include the external public relations department, the business department that communicates with customers, the general affairs department that reports to the government and supervisory ministries, etc.) | not_started | — |
| 19-4-c | Clarify the roles and responsibilities of stakeholders both during an event (incident) and in normal time | not_started | — |
| 19-4-d | Develop an incident response team that is available 24 hours a day, 365 days a year, physically or remotely | not_started | — |
| 19-4-e | Periodically review your organization's members, and their roles and responsibilities | not_started | — |

## Insufficient Measures Risks

In the absence of a predefined procedure for incident response, ad hoc response to emergencies and incidents might run into rework and redoing of steps, because of a lack of clarity as to the personnel involved and their roles and responsibilities.
